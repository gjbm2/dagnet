## Purpose

73m fixes the first half of the 73h carrier problem: `carrier_to_x` becomes a real composed runtime object rather than a stale path-scalar mixture. This plan fixes the second half: evidence that describes `A -> X` arrivals must bind to `carrier_to_x` under an explicit statistical contract.

The goal is not to rebuild the whole conditioned forecast system. The goal is to add a principled carrier-conditioning layer on top of the corrected carrier object, so the runtime can say exactly:

- what evidence was admitted for `carrier_to_x`;
- whether that evidence moved carrier timing, reach, both, or neither;
- how that conditioning interacts with subject-side conditioning;
- how projection reads the conditioned object without re-deciding semantics.

73h should not be considered fully closed until both 73m and this plan are addressed, or until a deliberate decision record says carrier evidence-conditioning is intentionally deferred with known consequences.

## Non-goals

This plan does not alter the meaning of the displayed rate. It remains `Y / X`.

It does not allow anchor-rooted whole-query numerator evidence to become subject evidence. Evidence for `A -> X` conditions `carrier_to_x`; evidence for `X -> end` conditions `subject_span` or subject rate.

It does not change the existing direct-cohort `p` conditioning seam. That seam remains about which evidence may move the subject rate, not about carrier timing.

It does not promote a gross-fitted whole-query numerator representation.

It does not solve 73l projection parity.

## Core distinction

Carrier composition and carrier conditioning are different.

Carrier composition answers: given resolved per-edge priors and topology, what is the prior arrival distribution from `A` to `X`?

Carrier conditioning answers: given observed arrivals at `X` for the selected population, how should the carrier prior be updated or selected?

The current system partially conditions carrier timing through empirical Tier 2 shape construction, but that is not the same statistical operation as subject-side IS conditioning. This plan makes that difference explicit and then replaces the implicit tier flip with a typed conditioning policy.

## Evidence role contract

Introduce an explicit carrier evidence role in the shared evidence model:

- role: `carrier_to_x_arrivals`;
- object conditioned: `carrier_to_x`;
- semantic question: for population rooted at `A`, who has arrived at denominator node `X` by age `tau`?
- admissible raw evidence family: observed `A -> X` arrivals on the anchor clock for the same selected population, date bounds, context, regime, and as-at boundary;
- forbidden evidence family: subject-end `Y` or `Z` observations, terminal-edge rows, and whole-query numerator rows unless separately admitted as exact carrier-arrival observations.

This role is distinct from:

- `window_subject_helper`, which conditions an `X -> end` subject helper;
- `direct_cohort_exact_subject`, which may condition subject rate for exact single-hop cohort subjects;
- Bayes Phase 1 and Phase 2 roles, which serve model fitting rather than live CF carrier conditioning.

For this plan, carrier evidence conditions the **whole `A -> X` carrier object** unless the evidence candidate explicitly carries a reviewed path identity. Edge-local rows for a terminal upstream edge are not automatically whole-carrier evidence in fan-in or multi-path topologies. If attribution to a specific path is missing, the candidate may only be admitted to the whole-carrier role when the adapter can prove the observation was generated by the same full `A -> X` selector as the runtime object.

## Carrier observation-shape taxonomy

Before any conditioning policy consumes evidence, every admitted carrier candidate must declare its observation shape. Counts alone are not enough.

Supported observation shapes are:

- `single_frontier_cumulative`: one observation at age `tau`, with `x_tau` arrivals at `X` out of the selected `A` population by that age;
- `trajectory_cumulative`: multiple cumulative observations for the same cohort day at multiple ages;
- `daily_increment`: arrivals into `X` during one age/day bin rather than cumulative arrivals by that age;
- `exact_member_arrival`: member-level or hash-level arrival facts that can be aggregated without cumulative double-counting.

`reconstructed_asat` is not a separate likelihood family. It is a provenance flag describing how evidence was materialised. The underlying observation shape remains one of the four shapes above, and the adapter must preserve that original shape.

Each shape has different likelihood and dedupe rules. In particular, cumulative rows at multiple ages for the same cohort day must not be treated as independent `single_frontier_cumulative` observations. They either become a trajectory likelihood or must be reduced to one explicitly chosen frontier observation with provenance.

If an adapter cannot classify the shape, the candidate is rejected as `unknown_carrier_observation_shape`.

## Statistical policy to decide

Before implementation, this plan requires a documented policy decision. There are three viable policies.

**Policy A — prior-only carrier with evidence provenance.**  
Observed carrier evidence is admitted and reported, but does not move the carrier object. This is conservative and easy to reason about, but it leaves 73h Issue 2 unresolved except as an explicit deferral.

**Policy B — empirical timing shape, topological reach.**  
Observed carrier evidence conditions the carrier timing CDF, while eventual reach remains the graph-topological reach. This matches the practical intuition behind the existing Tier 2 path but makes it typed, scoped, and auditable. It is a partial conditioning policy: timing moves, reach does not.

**Policy C — IS-conditioned carrier particles.**  
The carrier prior supplies per-draw arrival CDFs. Observed `A -> X` arrival evidence supplies a likelihood over those draws. The carrier particle set is reweighted and resampled in the same style as the subject-side conditioning path. This is the cleanest invariant-1 answer, but it is the broadest implementation.

Recommended path: implement Policy B first only if it is explicitly labelled as a staged policy, not as full unification. Then evaluate whether Policy C is necessary from residual failures and diagnostics. If the project owner wants full 73g invariant-1 symmetry immediately, choose Policy C and skip Policy B.

Policy B does **not** close the machinery-symmetry part of 73h Issue 2 by itself. It can close only a narrower, reviewed claim: carrier evidence is bound to the correct object and affects timing under a named partial policy. Marking 73h Issue 2 fully closed under Policy B requires an explicit architecture waiver accepting empirical timing replacement as the durable carrier-conditioning design.

## Mathematical invariants

Carrier evidence may move the distribution of arrival time at `X`. It must not move the subject rate directly.

If reach remains topological, the conditioned carrier CDF must remain conditional on eventual arrival at `X`. Projection multiplies reach when it needs absolute denominator mass.

If a future policy allows evidence to move reach, the plan must define whether reach is a graph prior, an inferred probability, or a posterior expectation. Until that decision exists, reach must remain topological.

Observed carrier evidence may imply an eventual arrival fraction different from topological reach. Under Policy B this conflict is not resolved by changing reach. It must be surfaced in provenance as `empirical_reach_conflict` with both the topological reach and the empirical terminal fraction where available.

For Policy C, the carrier-side likelihood follows the same parametrisation choice as the resolved 73k subject-side form: completeness/reach enters the success-probability slot, not the trial-count slot. For a `single_frontier_cumulative` observation with selected anchor population `A_i`, observed arrivals `x_i`, carrier reach `r_s`, and conditional carrier CDF `C_AX,s(tau_i)` for draw `s`, the likelihood shape is `x_i ~ Binomial(A_i, r_s * C_AX,s(tau_i))`. The trial count is the selected anchor population. Implementations must not silently substitute `Binomial(A_i * r_s, C_AX,s(tau_i))`.

Subject-side likelihoods must not consume carrier evidence as if it were subject-end evidence. Carrier evidence has the wrong object and clock for subject-rate conditioning.

Carrier and subject conditioning may both operate in one forecast, but they must produce two conditioned objects:

- conditioned `carrier_to_x`;
- conditioned `subject_span` or subject-rate object.

Projection reads their combination; it does not create a third conditioning story.

If carrier and subject uncertainty are both represented by particle families, conditioning must state the draw-coupling rule before any resampling occurs. Independent carrier and subject reweights are allowed only when the subject likelihood is conditionally independent of carrier timing, such as `window()` or `A = X` identity carriers. For active `cohort(A != X)` evidence whose likelihood uses carrier-to-subject path completeness, carrier and subject evidence must be combined in one joint particle likelihood and one joint resample unless a reviewed alternative proves the same posterior factorisation. Reusing indices from separately resampled carrier and subject arrays is invalid, and projection cannot repair that posterior after the fact.

## Stage 0 — Decide and record the carrier policy

Stage 0 chooses Policy A, B, or C for the first implementation.

The decision record must state:

- whether evidence can move carrier timing;
- whether evidence can move reach;
- whether conditioning is empirical replacement, IS reweighting, or no-op with provenance;
- which observation shapes are admitted for the first implementation;
- whether evidence conditions the whole `A -> X` carrier or path-specific carriers;
- the draw-coupling rule if particles are used;
- whether subject evidence is independent of carrier timing or requires joint carrier/subject reweighting;
- the evidence denominator used by any subject-side IS likelihood that combines carrier and subject timing;
- the closure language allowed for the chosen policy, including whether 73h Issue 2 remains partially open;
- the stricter admission criteria for selector-only population identity, including required regime equality, supported observation shapes, and any minimum support threshold;
- the sparse-data regularisation rule for empirical timing if Policy B is selected;
- the threshold for `empirical_reach_conflict`;
- which tests are expected to move;
- which residual defects remain out of scope.

Stop condition: no production code changes until this policy is written down.

## Stage 1 — Extend the shared evidence role model

Stage 1 extends the typed evidence layer with a carrier role.

The new role must include the identity dimensions needed to prove evidence belongs to `carrier_to_x`:

- mode and time origin, including whether the source is `window()` or `cohort()`;
- anchor node and population root;
- denominator node `X`;
- selected cohort date bounds and anchor-day set where applicable;
- selected cohort set or exact-member population identity when available;
- context and case identity;
- regime or hash-family identity;
- as-at boundary;
- temporal evidence basis and observation clock, explicitly anchor-relative for cohort carrier evidence.
- weighting or aggregation procedure used to collapse the evidence.
- observation shape from the taxonomy above;
- carrier scope, either whole `A -> X` or a reviewed path identity.
- source semantic role, and any source subject end or subject span carried by the row, so subject/numerator rows can be rejected rather than silently reinterpreted as carrier arrivals.

The merge output must expose raw carrier evidence totals, included observations, skipped observations, source totals, shape counts, carrier scope, and compact provenance. Candidates whose population identity is selector-only rather than exact-member identity must carry that distinction in provenance. Stage 0 must define the stricter admission criteria for selector-only candidates before they can be admitted.

Stop condition: pure merge tests prove carrier evidence is admitted only for the matching `A -> X` role and rejected when it is actually subject or numerator evidence, wrong carrier scope, unknown observation shape, or insufficient population identity.

## Stage 2 — Carrier evidence candidate adapters

Stage 2 adds thin adapters that convert existing snapshot, file-backed, and reconstructed-as-at observations into carrier evidence candidates.

Adapters may know current row shapes. They must not make the final admission decision and must not sum evidence outside the shared merge library.

Minimum provenance carried by each candidate:

- source kind;
- anchor identity;
- denominator node;
- observed age or observed date on the carrier clock;
- retrieved-at or as-at materialisation marker;
- context and regime identity;
- selected population identity or selector hash;
- counts representing arrival at `X`, not arrival at the subject end.
- observation shape and whether cumulative rows are part of a trajectory group;
- carrier scope proof: whole-carrier selector match or explicit path identity.
- reconstructed-as-at provenance, when applicable, without replacing the underlying observation shape.

Stop condition: adapter tests prove that a row describing `Y` or `Z` arrival cannot be converted into carrier evidence by accident, and that edge-local rows are rejected as whole-carrier evidence in fan-in or multi-path topologies unless they carry a valid whole-carrier selector.

## Stage 3 — Prepare carrier evidence in the runtime bundle

Stage 3 adds carrier evidence preparation beside existing subject evidence preparation.

For one forecast subject, preparation should now produce:

- `carrier_to_x` prior object from 73m;
- carrier evidence set for `carrier_to_x`;
- subject-span object;
- subject evidence set or existing `p_conditioning_evidence`;
- provenance for both evidence roles.

The runtime bundle must keep carrier evidence separate from `p_conditioning_evidence`. Do not overload the existing subject evidence object with carrier fields.

Stop condition: diagnostics for a cohort query can show carrier evidence and subject evidence side by side, with different roles and clocks.

## Stage 4 — Implement the selected conditioning policy

Stage 4 implements the policy chosen in Stage 0.

For Policy A, attach evidence and provenance only. The conditioned carrier equals the prior carrier. Tests must prove the no-op is intentional and visible.

For Policy B, build a carrier timing CDF from admitted arrival observations and expose it as the conditioned carrier timing shape. The construction must be shape-aware:

- `single_frontier_cumulative` may contribute one cumulative point;
- `trajectory_cumulative` must be handled as one trajectory object or deliberately reduced to one frontier point;
- `daily_increment` must be accumulated before becoming a CDF;
- `exact_member_arrival` may build an empirical CDF directly;
- reconstructed-as-at evidence follows its preserved original observation shape.

Reach remains topological. The carrier object must record that timing was empirically conditioned and reach was not. If the empirical terminal fraction conflicts materially with topological reach, the conditioned object must expose that conflict rather than hiding it. Stage 0 must choose the sparse-data rule for empirical timing: minimum support threshold, smoothing/regularisation method, or explicit rejection of sparse empirical conditioning.

Under Policy B, the conditioned carrier timing shape becomes a deterministic input to any later subject-side IS pass for the same forecast unless Stage 0 explicitly defines a particle representation for empirical carrier timing. This sequencing must be visible in diagnostics: carrier timing was selected or regularised first, subject evidence then conditions subject rate/span against that carrier timing, and reach remains topological throughout. Policy B must not pretend to have sampled a joint carrier/subject posterior.

For Policy C, the IS proposal is the combined carrier/subject particle state whenever both evidence roles are active and subject evidence depends on carrier timing. The carrier part is 73m's composed prior carrier MC draw set (`carrier_to_x.mc_cdf` plus the corresponding reach draw or fixed reach policy). The subject part is the prepared subject-span particle family, including span-level probability and timing. The implementation must build one joint log-likelihood from admitted carrier evidence plus admitted subject evidence, use one ESS-targeted tempering decision, and perform one joint resample of the combined state. Carrier-only reweighting is allowed only when no subject evidence is active for the solve, and independent subject-only reweighting is allowed only when the subject likelihood is independent of carrier timing.

The carrier evidence likelihood must match the observation shape:

- for `single_frontier_cumulative`, use a single cumulative-arrival likelihood at the frontier age;
- for `trajectory_cumulative`, use a product-of-conditional-Binomials trajectory likelihood, matching the Bayes compiler's cumulative-trajectory treatment unless a reviewed decision explicitly chooses a different likelihood;
- for `daily_increment`, use increment-bin likelihoods;
- for `exact_member_arrival`, use the member-level or aggregated arrival-time likelihood;
- for reconstructed-as-at evidence, use the preserved original shape's likelihood.

The subject evidence likelihood must use the evidence-completeness contract from 73m: span-level subject probability for `X -> end`, the matching subject or path completeness object for the evidence denominator, and no terminal-edge substitution in multi-hop. If the subject likelihood uses active carrier timing, it is part of the Policy C joint likelihood rather than a second pass.

Stop condition: the carrier object records prior-vs-conditioned provenance, tests can distinguish an unconditioned carrier from a conditioned one, observation-shape-specific tests prove cumulative evidence is not double-counted, and particle tests prove that active-carrier subject evidence is jointly reweighted rather than independently resampled.

## Stage 5 — Integrate with population projection

Stage 5 wires the conditioned carrier object into the existing population projection.

Projection uses the conditioned carrier timing shape for denominator arrival at `X`. It uses the subject object for progression from `X` to the subject end. It does not let carrier evidence move the subject rate.

The all-non-latency carrier case remains a guardrail: if carrier timing is a Dirac-at-zero shape, carrier evidence should not create a delayed carrier unless the admitted evidence genuinely says arrivals to `X` are delayed and the chosen policy allows evidence to move timing.

Under `empirical_reach_conflict`, projection uses the canonical topological-reach-saturated carrier object unless Stage 0 selected a reach-inference policy. The conflict is surfaced in provenance for diagnostic review and does not auto-reconcile by changing reach or by scaling displayed rates. Stage 0 may instead choose to reject empirical timing when the reach conflict exceeds the selected threshold.

For Policy C, Pop C numerator projection uses the same carrier-arrival and subject-progression convolution as 73m, but with the conditioned carrier draw family and the conditioned subject draw family from the joint particle state. Deterministic summaries must be derived from that conditioned state or from a documented deterministic projection of it, not from the prior carrier timing.

Stop condition: count fields move when carrier timing evidence moves; displayed rates move only through the mathematically implied denominator/numerator projection, not by direct reach or evidence injection into subject `p`.

For particle-based conditioning, projection tests must prove the selected carrier/subject draw-coupling rule is used consistently in rates and bands, and that projection does not pair separately resampled carrier and subject arrays by matching indices after the fact.

## Stage 6 — Cross-surface provenance

Stage 6 exposes compact carrier-conditioning provenance wherever CF diagnostics already expose subject-conditioning provenance. This extends the 73m Stage 6 diagnostic schema in the same payload object; CF responses must not emit one provenance block for carrier composition and a separate, disconnected block for carrier conditioning.

The response-visible provenance should identify:

- carrier evidence role;
- raw carrier evidence totals;
- source totals;
- skipped counts by reason;
- selected conditioning policy;
- mode and population root;
- carrier scope;
- temporal evidence basis and weighting or aggregation procedure;
- observation-shape counts;
- whether timing moved;
- whether reach moved;
- draw-coupling rule when particles are present;
- evidence denominator used by any subject/path completeness likelihood;
- empirical reach conflict if present;
- as-at boundary;
- scenario scope.

Full row-level provenance may remain diagnostic-only, but tests must be able to inspect it.

Stop condition: a CF response or diagnostic payload can explain both carrier and subject evidence binding without reading logs.

## Stage 7 — Acceptance tests

Required tests:

Regression discipline for `graph-editor/lib/tests/test_cohort_factorised_outside_in.py` is strict. The 73m Stage 0 baseline remains the reference unless this plan records a new baseline before implementation. Carrier evidence-conditioning may close additional cases, but it must not introduce new failures in that module, must not xfail or skip existing passing tests, and must not relax assertions to hide changed semantics. If a test expectation must change because carrier evidence is now correctly admitted, the plan or decision record must name the carrier evidence role, observation shape, conditioning policy, and projection object that justify the change.

- pure merge tests for `carrier_to_x_arrivals`;
- adapter tests rejecting wrong-clock and wrong-object rows;
- runtime-bundle tests showing separate carrier and subject evidence objects;
- policy tests for the selected conditioning behaviour;
- projection tests proving reach/rate separation still holds;
- tests proving carrier evidence identity rejects wrong mode, wrong temporal basis, and wrong aggregation procedure;
- integration tests on a cohort query where observed `A -> X` arrival timing is materially different from the carrier prior;
- integration tests on a fan-in or multi-path carrier proving edge-local evidence is not admitted as whole-carrier evidence;
- tests where empirical terminal mass conflicts with topological reach, proving the conflict is surfaced;
- regression tests for `window()` and `A = X`, where carrier evidence conditioning should naturally vanish or be identity.

If Policy B is selected, add a test proving reach remains topological even when empirical timing shape wins.

If Policy C is selected, add tests for weak-evidence and strong-evidence regimes so the carrier posterior behaves sensibly rather than collapsing or ignoring evidence. Also add an active-carrier subject-evidence test proving carrier and subject particles are jointly reweighted in one pass when the subject likelihood depends on path completeness.

For any policy that admits cumulative multi-age evidence, add a test proving the implementation does not treat repeated cumulative retrievals for one cohort day as independent frontier observations.

## Stage 8 — Closure criteria for 73h Issue 2

73h Issue 2 can be called closed only when the system has one explicit story for both sides:

- `carrier_to_x` is composed and conditioned according to a named policy;
- `subject_span` and subject rate are composed and conditioned according to their named policy;
- evidence roles are typed and auditable;
- projection reads the two resolved objects without choosing semantics again.

If the chosen first policy is Policy A or B, closure must say "composition fixed, carrier conditioning policy intentionally partial" unless a separate review accepts that partial policy as the durable design.

Policy B closure language must not say "same conditioning machinery as subject-side IS" unless Policy C or an equivalent particle-conditioning policy has landed.

Closure also requires that legacy Tier 2 dispatcher paths are either retired or re-routed through the typed carrier evidence adapter path. Coexistence between the legacy Tier 2 path and the typed path is not a closure state.

## Review checklist

Reviewers should reject an implementation if:

- carrier evidence is represented as loose tuples without role identity;
- subject-end evidence can enter the carrier role;
- carrier-arrival evidence can enter subject-rate conditioning;
- reach moves without an explicit reach-inference policy;
- empirical timing silently replaces prior timing without provenance;
- cumulative carrier rows are double-counted as independent observations;
- edge-local rows are admitted as whole-carrier evidence in fan-in or multi-path carriers without proof;
- evidence with the wrong mode, temporal basis, or aggregation procedure is admitted;
- Policy C performs separate carrier and subject resamples when subject evidence depends on active carrier timing;
- carrier and subject particles are paired by reused indices after separate resampling;
- selector-only population identity is treated as exact member identity;
- projection multiplies displayed rates by reach;
- `window()` or `A = X` stops degenerating to identity carrier semantics;
- diagnostics cannot explain which evidence conditioned which object.
